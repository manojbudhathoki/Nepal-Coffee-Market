From a small beginning to a national identity

Nepal's coffee story is often traced to the introduction of coffee plants in the Gulmi area in the mid-20th century. From there, cultivation gradually spread to suitable mid-hill regions, including parts of Gulmi, Palpa, Syangja, Kaski, Lalitpur, Kavrepalanchok, and Ilam.

Smallholder farmers, cooperatives, local processors, and roasters have helped shape the sector. Nepal's varied elevations, local growing conditions, and careful small-batch work can create distinctive coffees, although flavour and quality vary by farm, variety, processing, and roast.

As domestic coffee culture grows, Nepali coffee is finding new audiences in cafés, homes, and specialty markets abroad. Sustainable growth depends on fair relationships with farmers, transparent sourcing, consistent quality, and accurate origin information.
